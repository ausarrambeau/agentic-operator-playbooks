# Turn one real receipt into one honest post

Strong content does not begin with a viral hook. It begins with something real
you can show: a build, test, decision, failure, before-and-after state, or
lesson you earned.

The goal is one clear draft where:

- the audience recognizes a problem;
- the opening makes one promise;
- owned proof resolves that promise;
- the body teaches one useful move; and
- the call to action leads somewhere that actually exists.

**If you are new to this:** use **Your first ten-minute task** to turn one
recent piece of work into `content-brief.md`. Do not try to plan a month of
posts.

## The content loop

### 1. Collect owned proof

Choose evidence you own or directly produced:

- a working screen or artifact;
- a test result;
- a visible state change;
- a decision and its reasoning;
- a failure and the repair;
- a document or workflow you can show; or
- a bounded result with a clear source.

If the proof does not exist yet, make the artifact before promising the story.
Do not borrow another creator’s result or authority.

### 2. Name the human consequence

Explain why the proof matters in ordinary language.

Useful consequences include:

- less repeated work;
- fewer missed details;
- a faster decision;
- an error caught before it became expensive;
- a clearer handoff; or
- one task becoming easier to repeat.

The consequence is the story. The tool is supporting detail.

### 3. Choose one audience and one promise

Write:

- **Audience:** who has this problem?
- **Problem:** what are they experiencing?
- **Promise:** what will they understand or be able to try after this post?

One post should resolve one promise. Do not stack a tutorial, founder story,
product launch, and opinion into the same short piece.

### 4. Draft three original hooks

Create three openings that make the same promise from different angles:

1. **Consequence first** — the cost or relief in human terms.
2. **Proof first** — the visible result or test.
3. **Tension first** — the assumption that failed or the error that was caught.

Choose the hook whose promised payoff your owned proof can resolve fastest.
Do not imitate another creator’s identity, catchphrases, or personal story.

### 5. Build the proof sequence

A simple short-form sequence is:

1. Problem or tension.
2. What you tried.
3. The proof on screen.
4. What changed or what failed.
5. The one useful move the viewer can take.

Show the receipt at the moment the claim needs support. A claim that appears
before its proof creates curiosity; a claim that never reaches proof breaks
trust.

### 6. Keep claims inside the evidence

Separate:

- **Observed:** what the artifact, test, or result directly shows.
- **Inferred:** what you think the observation means.
- **Unknown:** what has not been measured.

Do not turn views, likes, a single example, or someone else’s claim into proof
of causality or guaranteed results.

### 7. Choose a real call to action

The next step must exist before the post promises it.

Examples:

- try the prompt shown;
- read a linked playbook;
- reply with a specific question;
- follow for the next verified step; or
- request an asset that already has a delivery path.

If the asset or delivery path is missing, use a simpler CTA. Never promise a
send, download, or result you cannot fulfil.

### 8. Review before publishing

Run a wall check:

- Does every factual claim have proof?
- Is the opening resolved?
- Is the premise original?
- Is private or identifying information hidden?
- Can the CTA actually be fulfilled?
- Is the draft useful without inflated numbers?
- Does it still sound like you?

Drafting is reversible. Publishing is external action and waits for your
approval.

## Your first ten-minute task

Paste this into your agent:

```text
Help me turn one real piece of work into one honest content draft.

OWNED PROOF:
[Describe or attach the build, test, decision, failure, result, or artifact]

AUDIENCE:
[Who this is useful for]

FORMAT:
[Short video, carousel, text post, email, or "recommend one"]

DESTINATION:
[The real next step or asset, or "none yet"]

Work in this order:
1. Inspect the proof. State what it directly shows, what would be inference,
   and what remains unknown.
2. Name the human consequence in one sentence.
3. Write one audience, one problem, and one promise.
4. Draft three original hooks: consequence first, proof first, and tension
   first. For each, name the proof beat that resolves it.
5. Choose the strongest supported hook and write one short draft with:
   opening, proof sequence, useful takeaway, and CTA.
6. If the destination or delivery path does not exist, replace the CTA with a
   simple honest next step.
7. Save the result as content-brief.md.

Rules:
- Use only proof I own or can directly show.
- Do not copy another creator's wording, identity, story, or visual brand.
- Do not invent numbers, outcomes, testimonials, or authority.
- Protect private and identifying information.
- Do not publish, post, send, schedule, or message anyone.
- Stop after one draft.
```

The saved `content-brief.md` is your first receipt.

## The reusable content prompt

Use this after the brief has real proof:

```text
Act as my evidence-first content operator. Turn one owned receipt into an
original, publish-ready draft without crossing the publication gate.

OWNED RECEIPT:
[Artifact, screen, result, decision, failure, or test]

AUDIENCE AND PROBLEM:
[Who it helps and what they feel]

FORMAT AND LIMITS:
[Format, length, voice, platform constraints]

CTA DESTINATION:
[Existing asset or next step]

Work in this order:
1. Audit the receipt. Separate observed facts, inference, and unknowns.
2. State the human consequence and the single promise.
3. Produce three original hooks and pair each with the exact proof that
   resolves it.
4. Select one hook by proof fit, not by unsupported claims about virality.
5. Build a short sequence: tension, attempt, visible proof, consequence,
   useful move, CTA.
6. Draft the piece in my voice using fresh language.
7. Create a shot or evidence list that names what must appear on screen and
   what must be hidden.
8. Run a final review for:
   - unsupported claims;
   - copied phrasing or borrowed identity;
   - unresolved opening;
   - private information;
   - missing CTA asset or delivery path; and
   - a claim stronger than the proof.
9. Repair real failures and return:
   - final draft;
   - proof map;
   - privacy/redaction list;
   - CTA readiness; and
   - remaining blockers.

Boundaries:
- Draft and review only.
- Do not publish, schedule, send, comment, or message.
- Do not manufacture proof or imply guaranteed performance.
- If the owned receipt is missing, stop and tell me what artifact must exist
  first.
```

## Common failure modes

- Starting with a hook before finding owned proof.
- Making the tool the story instead of the human consequence.
- Combining several promises into one piece.
- Copying a creator’s phrasing or identity.
- Treating attention metrics as proof of business impact.
- Hiding uncertainty to make the claim stronger.
- Promising an asset or DM delivery path that does not exist.
- Letting an agent publish because it was allowed to draft.

## Done means

- `content-brief.md` contains one audience, problem, promise, and owned proof.
- Three hooks are original and each has a proof beat.
- The selected draft resolves its opening.
- Observations, inferences, and unknowns stay distinct.
- Private information has a redaction plan.
- The CTA has a real destination.
- The piece remains a draft until you approve publication.
